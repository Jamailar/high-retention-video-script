# Product Script Taxonomy

Classify the product before writing. Product type decides what proof matters, which beats are risky, and what CTA is credible. Then classify the video job, trust mode, and delivery format. These are separate decisions: an app can be taught through a screen-led tutorial, sold through a founder-led case story, or introduced through a motion-led reveal.

Classify the viewer's decision at the same time. The product type tells the creator what to show; the viewer moment tells the creator why that proof matters now. Do not let feature completeness replace relevance.

## Presentation Architecture Gate

Choose presentation architecture before looking for a story. Story is one option, not the default wrapper for every video.

| Viewer intent | Default architecture | Opening obligation | Narrative budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Learn or activate a utility | Result-first demonstration | Show the finished artifact or successful action in the first beat, normally 0-8 seconds; a useful cited proof/source may occupy that beat only when the result follows immediately | No backstory; at most one short problem sentence after proof |
| Understand a new tool | Result -> definition -> live workflow -> output -> limitation -> action | Make the tool and its result visually identifiable immediately | Origin story only when explicitly requested |
| Evaluate a purchase or choice | Decision-first comparison | State the decision and criteria, then show evidence | Personal experience only as evidence |
| Learn a mechanism | Answer or proof first | Give a useful answer before extended context | Story only when it makes the mechanism clearer |
| Follow a case, experiment, or transformation | Story, test, or case architecture | Enter the consequence, question, or evidence | Full narrative is allowed because it is the proof engine |

Hard rules:

- “Human” does not mean autobiographical. Direct, specific instruction can be fully human.
- Do not add a confession, failed first attempt, false start, conflict, or reversal unless it answers the viewer's actual question.
- A tool tutorial fails when the viewer must watch creator setup before seeing what the tool produces.
- The user's requested format wins. If the request says “introduction,” “tutorial,” “how to use,” or “show the result,” default to direct utility structure unless they also ask for a case story or origin story.

## Audience-First Product Questions

| Product type | Viewer situation | Likely exit risk | Belief the video must earn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SaaS / app tutorial | Trying to finish a task with a fragmented workflow | Looks like another menu tour | I can complete my real job faster or better |
| AI tool / agent | Disappointed by generic output or unreliable automation | Feels like a staged magic demo | It uses real context and produces inspectable work |
| Physical product | Comparing use in a real environment | Looks polished but hides limitations | It works under conditions like mine |
| Course / service | Unsure whether promised expertise is concrete | Free content has no useful substance | I learned something useful before being asked to buy |
| Developer tool | Under integration, reliability, or maintenance pressure | Setup truth and failure modes are hidden | The minimal path works and the tradeoffs are explicit |
| Creator/media product | Overloaded by tools and inconsistent output | More features, same workload | The workflow creates a reusable asset or result |

## Product Types

| Product type | Primary viewer question | Best proof | Script bias | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SaaS / app tutorial | Can I actually use this to finish my job? | Real workflow, before/after, generated artifact | Show outcome before setup | Menu tour |
| AI tool / agent | Will it use real context and avoid generic output? | Source input, reasoning artifact, final output, revision | Show input -> intermediate -> output | Magic demo with no evidence |
| Physical product | Does it work in my real environment? | Hands-on demo, durability, comparison, close-ups | Show tactile proof and failure cases | Over-polished lifestyle shots |
| Course / info product | Will I learn a specific capability? | Lesson sample, student result, framework | Teach one useful slice before selling | Vague transformation claim |
| Service / agency | Can I trust this provider? | Case result, process, diagnostic, client constraints | Show how decisions are made | Talking only about credentials |
| Community / membership | Will people like me get value here? | Member activity, rituals, examples, outcomes | Show belonging and cadence | Empty hype |
| Marketplace / ecommerce | Why this item and why now? | Comparison, selection criteria, price/value, review signals | Make the decision easier | SKU dump |
| Developer tool / API | Does it integrate cleanly and save engineering time? | Code path, logs, docs, error handling, performance | Show minimal working path | Skipping setup truth |
| Local venue / event | Is it worth leaving home for? | Real scene, route, queue, price, experience | Show sensory and practical details | Overly cinematic but unhelpful |
| Creator/media product | Will this improve my content output? | Actual produced asset, workflow reuse, analytics | Show artifact and reuse loop | Only showing features |

## Conversion Jobs

Pick one:

- Awareness: make the viewer understand the problem and remember the product.
- Consideration: prove the product is credible and relevant.
- Activation: help the viewer complete first success.
- Upgrade: show a deeper use case or compounding value.
- Re-engagement: show what changed or what they missed.

Pick one primary job and at most one secondary job. If awareness, activation, conversion, and upgrade all appear equally important, the script has no decision hierarchy.

## Trust Modes And Delivery Formats

The presenter and visual form must match what the audience trusts for this message.

| Trust mode | Best for | Credible delivery | Failure pattern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peer / real user | Relatable recommendation, everyday use, UGC | Real setting, direct language, imperfect but clear capture | Polished studio performance pretending to be spontaneous |
| Practitioner | Tutorial, workflow, tool review, case experience | Screen or hands-on proof with informed commentary | Generic endorsement with no real operation |
| Founder / builder | Product reasoning, launch, vision plus proof | Personal stakes, product truth, visible artifacts and limitations | Biography or brand story before viewer value |
| Expert / authority | High-consideration technical, financial, health, B2B, or educational content | Clear credentials, evidence, caveats, precise language | Casual unsupported certainty or costume-like authority |
| Customer / case subject | Transformation and outcome proof | Specific before/after, constraints, process, permission | Vague testimonial or staged result |
| Entertainer / host | Challenge, experiment, broad discovery | Strong story engine, reactions, stakes, repeatable format | Entertainment disconnected from the promised product value |

| Delivery format | Choose when | Required production proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Screen-led | UI action and result are the main evidence | Readable screen states, cursor path, start/action/end, real output |
| Presenter-led | Trust, interpretation, story, or emotional connection matters | Cast, framing, delivery, clean audio, gesture, eyeline, B-roll plan |
| Hybrid presenter + screen | Both human trust and inspectable operation matter | Exact A-roll/B-roll handoffs and valid source IDs |
| Voiceover-led | Visual process or product is primary and presenter presence is unnecessary | Complete audio spine, visual proof for every claim, caption plan |
| UGC / testimonial | Peer trust and lived experience are the message | Realistic setting, specific experience, disclosure/permission, product use |
| Interview / case | Another person's story or expertise carries proof | Questions, reaction beats, release/permission, edit context |
| Motion / animated | Abstract mechanism, data, or product reveal needs controlled visualization | Exact scene timing, final copy, asset list, animation purpose |

Selection rules:

- Use screen-led or hybrid for app tutorials; do not hide the product behind talking-head footage.
- Use presenter-led only when the person adds trust, interpretation, emotion, or authorship.
- Use UGC only when the tone and claim genuinely support peer credibility.
- Use motion to clarify or stage a reveal, not to replace missing product proof.
- Use a proven video/rendering library for programmatic animation, captions, timelines, transcription, or editing math when implementation is required. Do not hand-roll a media engine inside the script skill.

## Proof Ladder

Use the strongest available proof. Prefer higher proof levels when claims are important.

1. Real output artifact.
2. Before/after comparison.
3. Live screen recording or physical demo.
4. User/customer quote or comment.
5. Data point with context.
6. Process transparency.
7. Founder/expert claim.

If the script makes a strong claim but only has proof level 7, weaken the claim or add proof.

Create a claim-proof contract before scripting important lines:

| claim_id | claim | consequence if false | required proof | available source | script wording | capture gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rules:

- Place proof near the claim.
- A polished interface is not proof of a successful workflow.
- A testimonial is not proof of a universal result.
- A speed or quality comparison needs stated conditions.
- Product limitations can increase trust when they help the correct audience decide.
- If the proof gap cannot be closed, narrow or remove the claim before packaging.

## Product-Type Beat Adjustments

### SaaS / App / AI Tool

Order:

1. Final artifact or successful action preview.
2. One-sentence product definition.
3. Entry point or invocation.
4. Real input.
5. Guided workflow with only necessary explanation.
6. Intermediate state when it helps comprehension or trust.
7. Final artifact in inspectable detail.
8. Limitation, objection, privacy, or failure boundary.
9. Reuse, integration, or first action.

A broken workflow or pain point is optional. Use no more than one short sentence after the result unless diagnosing that problem is the video's primary educational job. Setup appears at the moment it is needed, never as a prelude to value.

Required rows:

- Privacy masking row.
- Real data input row.
- Failure/objection row.
- Final artifact row.

### Physical Product

Order:

1. Real use situation.
2. Immediate tactile proof.
3. Feature only after proof.
4. Comparison or stress test.
5. Buying decision.

Required rows:

- Close-up detail.
- In-use shot.
- Scale/size context.
- Limitation or fit warning.

### Course / Service

Order:

1. Painful mistake.
2. Teach one slice.
3. Show framework.
4. Show result/case.
5. Invite to next step.

Required rows:

- Useful free lesson.
- Specific audience fit.
- Who should not buy/use.
- Credible proof.

### Developer Tool

Order:

1. Broken developer workflow.
2. Minimal working example.
3. Integration path.
4. Logs/result.
5. Failure handling.
6. Docs/CTA.

Required rows:

- Command/code snippet.
- Error or edge case.
- Performance/security note.
- Linkable next action.
