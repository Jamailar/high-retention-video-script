# Ideation, Packaging, And Titles

Use this reference before writing a detailed script. A mature creator does not polish the first topic sentence that appears. The creator first chooses the strongest audience angle, then designs the title, thumbnail or first frame, hook, and payoff as one package.

## Research Basis

This workflow synthesizes useful patterns from:

- YouTube's official performance guidance: ideation and packaging affect appeal; the opening must immediately fulfil the expectation set by the title and thumbnail. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650
- YouTube's native A/B testing: up to three title, thumbnail, or combined variants can be compared, with watch time used to choose the winner. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400
- The open `creator-stack` plan-video, title, hook, and YouTube script skills: research before packaging, multiple options, user selection, title/visual complementarity, and hook continuation. Source: https://github.com/kenneth-liao/ai-launchpad-marketplace/tree/main/creator-stack/skills
- The open Indy Hall video-script skill: find one lesson, one concrete story, then work backward to the hook. Source: https://gist.github.com/alexknowshtml/6a1e4d336a6d51c6231bd6bd9a3f0d17
- Local creator, copywriting, strategy, and visual-direction workflows: generate several routes, explain tradeoffs, select one, and keep title and visual hierarchy aligned.

Borrow decision systems, not formulas blindly. Never force sensational framing onto a quiet brand, technical proof, sensitive topic, or audience that values precision.

## The Three Decisions

Keep these separate until selection:

1. **Topic:** the broad subject area.
2. **Angle:** the specific tension, result, question, test, or story that makes the topic worth watching now.
3. **Package:** the title, thumbnail or first frame, hook direction, and promised payoff that sells the selected angle.

Weak process:

`topic -> first title -> full script`

Required process:

`creator truth + audience evidence + proof engine -> selected content angle -> package candidates -> selected package -> human rough draft`

Do not generate a package from the product category alone. The title and hook must sample the strongest real conflict, result, judgment, test, or proof already present in the content. A hook can sharpen good material; it cannot make an empty feature list worth watching.

## Angle Exploration

Generate 3-7 genuinely different candidates. Change the viewer's reason to care, not only the wording.

| Angle family | Core question | Best when | Proof needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome / transformation | What changed, and can I get it? | A visible before/after exists | Real result and starting state |
| Mistake / hidden cost | What am I doing wrong or wasting? | Audience has a repeated failed workaround | Failure evidence and correction |
| Experiment / challenge | Will this work under clear rules? | The process can be tested on camera | Rules, attempt, result, limitation |
| Comparison / decision | Which option fits my situation? | Viewers are actively choosing | Fair criteria and side-by-side proof |
| Case story | What happened to this specific person or project? | A concrete moment contains change or conflict | Names/details with permission, or anonymized evidence |
| Counterintuitive mechanism | Why does the obvious method fail? | Product or method has a defensible insight | Mechanism and demonstration |
| Identity / emotional tension | Is this describing someone like me? | Pain or aspiration is socially recognizable | Real audience language and respectful handling |
| Teardown / reconstruction | Why did this work, and what can I reuse? | A reference or result can be analyzed | Source material and ethical adaptation |

### Angle Candidate Format

| field | requirement |
| --- | --- |
| `angle_name` | Short direction name |
| `viewer_tension` | Situation, desire, objection, or identity conflict |
| `single_lesson` | One actionable or perspective-changing takeaway |
| `promise` | Observable change by the end |
| `story_or_test` | Concrete engine that carries the video |
| `available_proof` | Evidence already available |
| `visual_engine` | What repeatedly gives the viewer something to see |
| `production_cost` | People, time, locations, product states, generation, rights |
| `risk` | Credibility, legal, privacy, brand, or platform risk |

## Angle Score

Score each candidate out of 100. Use evidence, not personal excitement.

| criterion | weight | question |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Audience relevance | 20 | Does this solve or illuminate a live viewer tension? |
| Promise strength | 15 | Is the end-state specific and meaningful? |
| Proof strength | 15 | Can important claims be demonstrated credibly? |
| Visual potential | 15 | Is there an ongoing visual engine, not only talking? |
| Novelty / contrast | 10 | Is there a fresh frame without becoming misleading? |
| Emotional force | 10 | Does the viewer feel stakes, relief, identity, or desire? |
| Production feasibility | 10 | Can it be captured at the required quality? |
| Platform fit | 5 | Does the angle suit placement, runtime, and viewer behavior? |

Selection rules:

- `85-100`: strong primary candidate.
- `75-84`: viable after one focused improvement.
- `<75`: do not move to detailed packaging unless alternatives are worse and the weakness is explicitly accepted.
- Reject any candidate with no credible proof path, no single lesson, or no visual engine for a visual platform.

Choose one primary angle and one backup. Record why the primary wins and why the other directions were not selected. This decision record can be summarized in the final HTML without showing every discarded draft.

## Title Exploration

Write at least 12 rough titles before shortlisting. Cover several families; do not generate 12 near-duplicates.

Useful title families:

- concrete outcome
- mistake or avoidable cost
- comparison or contrast
- experiment or challenge
- unexpected discovery
- specific case or moment
- mechanism or hidden cause
- audience identity or situation
- time, quantity, or constraint when true and meaningful

Each title must do four jobs:

1. Address the intended viewer or a problem they recognize.
2. Communicate enough value to make the click rational.
3. Raise one specific unanswered question.
4. Remain fully payable by the video and available proof.

Do not treat a title as a summary. Do not treat curiosity as permission to hide the topic.

### Search-Led Versus Browse-Led Titles

Choose the title logic from discovery context:

- **Search-led:** preserve the query and expected task, then add specificity, proof, or a meaningful distinction. Clarity outranks mystery.
- **Browse-led:** lead with a recognizable tension, consequence, result, contrast, or story. Curiosity may be stronger, but the subject must still be identifiable.
- **Short-form cover text:** use fewer words and let the first frame carry context. The spoken hook begins immediately; the cover cannot bear the whole promise.

Platform character counts are constraints, not a quality formula. Check current platform rules when exact limits matter.

## Package Construction

Reduce the rough titles to three complete package finalists. A finalist is not only a title.

| field | requirement |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | Final candidate wording |
| `thumbnail_or_first_frame` | One focal visual conflict, result, face, object, screen, or comparison |
| `thumbnail_text` | Optional; short and not duplicated by the title |
| `viewer_question` | The exact question the package creates |
| `promised_payoff` | What the viewer will receive |
| `proof_required` | What must appear for the promise to be credible |
| `opening_image` | The literal first visible state |
| `hook_direction` | How the opening adds new information |
| `risk` | Ambiguity, exaggeration, unsupported claim, brand or compliance concern |

Title, thumbnail, and first frame must divide the communication work:

- title names or frames the tension
- visual makes the result, contrast, stakes, or subject instantly legible
- hook confirms the package and escalates it with proof, conflict, or mechanism

Bad complementarity repeats the same sentence three times. Good complementarity creates one coherent narrative from three different pieces of information.

## Package Score

Score each finalist out of 100.

| criterion | weight | pass standard |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Viewer relevance | 20 | Intended viewer recognizes why this matters now |
| Clarity | 15 | Subject and value can be understood cold |
| Curiosity / tension | 15 | One concrete question remains unresolved |
| Credibility | 15 | Wording matches available proof and brand trust |
| Specificity | 10 | Uses a situation, result, constraint, number, or contrast when justified |
| Visual complementarity | 10 | Title and thumbnail/first frame add different information |
| Payoff fit | 10 | Script can fulfil the promise progressively and completely |
| Platform fit | 5 | Readable, native, and appropriate to discovery context |

Hard failures:

- Unsupported or misleading claim: reject, not merely deduct points.
- Title promises A while the video proves B: reject.
- Title and visual duplicate each other: revise before selection.
- More than one primary promise: narrow the package.
- Generic topic label with no viewer question: rewrite.

Selected package threshold: at least `80/100`, with no hard failure. Keep two diverse test variants rather than tiny word swaps.

## Package-To-Hook Contract

The first spoken and visual beat must:

1. show that the package promise is real
2. add information not already given by the title or image
3. create or sharpen the next question
4. establish proof, stakes, conflict, result, or relevance

Opening checks:

- No greeting before value.
- No exact title repetition.
- No unrelated origin story.
- No agenda narration such as “today I will introduce”.
- No vague claim that could introduce any product.
- No bait that is only repaid after a long delay.

The hook should remain understandable without the title or cover. A cold viewer should be able to identify the topic, the reason to stay for another ten seconds, and the source of credibility or proof. Reject generic pain -> benefits -> promise openings, fake viewer questions immediately answered by the narrator, and dramatic reversals unsupported by the actual video.

For tutorials, a quick result preview is often stronger than an explanation. For stories, enter a specific moment or consequence. For experiments, show the question, rules, or surprising result. For reviews, state the decision tension and test criteria.

For utility tutorials, treat delayed value as a hard opening failure: reject any version that spends the first beat on the creator's backstory, failed attempt, product origin, generic pain, or dramatic reversal before showing the tool's result. The result may be previewed quickly and revisited in detail later.

## Selection And Testing

If the user is collaborating live, present three finalists and a recommendation, then let them select or revise. If the user asked for autonomous execution, choose the highest-scoring package and mark it as the recommended selection with explicit assumptions.

For publishable variants:

- change a meaningful hypothesis: audience tension, consequence, story, result, or visual frame
- do not test punctuation or synonym swaps as if they were creative alternatives
- record what each variant is testing
- judge success using watch time or the creator's actual objective, not click-through rate alone

## Final HTML Decision Record

Keep only the selected package visible by default:

- selected angle and reason
- selected title, thumbnail/first frame, and hook
- viewer question and promised payoff
- package score and main risk
- why title, visual, and hook complement one another

Keep alternate package routes, all rough titles, and raw ideation notes internal unless the user asks for them.
